How Miiras helps

From a knot in your stomach to a conversation you're ready for.

1

Name what's worrying you

Say what's on your mind, even if it's messy. Miiras helps you understand what you're really worried about, and why it matters to you.

2

Start small

Pick a gentle question instead of one big talk. Ask in person, over dinner, or in a message you can send today.

3

Practice first

Rehearse out loud with a practice partner who can be open, brush it off, or get defensive, so the real talk feels familiar.

4

Keep what you learn

Note what you found out, what's still open, and the next small step, so each talk builds on the last.

Conversation decks

Be ready for whatever comes, one question at a time.

From retirement to health to what's written down. Start with how they feel, not the numbers, and go a little deeper with each short talk.

Deck 1Your retirement
"When do you picture yourself retiring, and do you feel you have a plan for it?"A gentle first question
Deck 2Money for later
"Do you feel your savings will cover what life costs once you stop working?"Feelings first, figures later
Deck 3If your health changes
"If you had a health scare, is there a plan for how bills and care would be handled?"So you're ready to help
Deck 4What's in writing
"Have you put anything in writing, like a will or trust, about what you'd want?"Best once you've talked a bit
Built on research

We listened to both generations first.

Before building anything, we sat down with parents and adult children to talk about money, family and the future. Here's what kept coming up.

“

Both generations said they'd welcome the conversation, if the other one started it.

Adult children mostly wanted to know they could help if something happened, not what they'd get.

Small questions get answered. Big talks get postponed.

A single, easy question often led to more than a planned sit-down ever did.
